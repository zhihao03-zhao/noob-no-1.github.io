I am a final-year Computer Science undergraduate at the National University of Singapore, with a minor in mathematics. I work on AI safety. My current research, advised by Prof. Min-Yen Kan, studies what happens to self-evolving agents in the domains their improvement signal does not measure, safety included. Before that I studied scaling laws for LLM post-training with Prof. Bryan Low.

I want to understand intelligence by building and taking apart artificial systems, and I am uneasy that we deploy models we can neither fully explain nor control. That keeps me close to alignment, interpretability and the training pipeline itself.

I am applying to Master's programmes for Fall 2027 and am open to research internships.

Research

  • 2026/07 – now
    agent safety

    What self-evolving agents lose while they improve

    Final-year project, NUS · Advisor: Prof. Min-Yen Kan

    A self-evolving agent improves against a reward or judge that sees only part of its behaviour. I evaluate training-based self-evolving systems, including AZR and SPIRAL, across reasoning, knowledge, instruction following and safety to find what shifts outside that signal. So far, reasoning gains carry over to neighbouring domains while safety tends to degrade.

    I am now testing whether rewards drawn from several domains at once can make self-improvement hold across all of them.

  • 2026/09 – now
    reward hacking

    Angry or Excited Agents: Do Emotional Contexts Drive Misaligned Actions?

    TARA fellowship group project

    We test whether a neutral, angry or excited user changes how often a model cheats or falsely reports success on impossible coding tasks from ImpossibleBench, and read out emotion directions from the activations of an open-weight model while it works.

  • 2025 – 2026/05
    post-training

    Conditional scaling laws for LLM post-training

    Undergraduate research (UROP), NUS · Advisor: Prof. Bryan Low

    How should a fixed data budget be split between supervised fine-tuning and DPO? Across 144 training runs on Llama-3.2 1B and 3B, evaluated on IFEval, MMLU and math, no single scaling law held across domains. The two stages interact non-additively: they cooperate at 3B and compete at 1B.
